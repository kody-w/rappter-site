Landing-page conversion proof for a synthetic field-service SaaS

A rendered landing-page proof with a sharper promise, evidence order, objection handling, call to action, and measurable conversion hypothesis.

The job

Customer problem

A small software company explains features clearly but does not connect them to one urgent buyer problem or a low-risk next action.

Synthetic inputs

Synthetic offer

A fictional scheduling product that helps small repair teams assign one owner to every appointment exception.

Synthetic buyer

An operations manager at a ten-person field-service company dealing with missed appointments and unclear handoffs.

Conversion constraint

The page may ask for a workflow review but cannot claim guaranteed savings, revenue, or appointment recovery.

What the AI did

  1. Reduced the page to one buyer, one costly problem, and one next action.
  2. Reordered evidence so the workflow and proof appear before the call to action.
  3. Translated feature language into observable operational outcomes.
  4. Added explicit objections, non-goals, and a low-risk synthetic proof step.
  5. Defined the events needed to measure whether the new structure improves qualified action.

Findings and deliverables

  • The strongest promise is exception ownership, not general scheduling automation.
  • A proof-before-integration offer reduces implementation anxiety without inventing customer results.
  • The page should separate measured evidence from expected value.
  • One primary call to action is clearer than competing demo, trial, newsletter, and contact buttons.

Acceptance checks

PASS

The hero names one buyer, problem, and outcome

PASS

What remains unproven

  • The proof does not measure real traffic, buyer comprehension, conversion, or product-market fit.
  • A production iteration would need approved brand language and privacy-safe aggregate funnel events.
